## 2. Derivations: how each new object was obtained

### 2.1 Octonion matrices $`\tau^{O}`$

Origin: the split matrices of 4+4.nb and the observation (checked) that $`\sigma=\tau_1\tau_2\tau_3=\tau_4\tau_5\tau_6\tau_7`$ commutes with $`\tau_1,\tau_2,\tau_3`$, anticommutes with $`\tau_4,\dots,\tau_7`$ and squares to $`1_8`$. Hence

```math
\begin{aligned}
\tau^{O}_0&=1_8,\quad \tau^{O}_A=\tau_A\ (A\le3),\quad \tau^{O}_k=\tau_k\sigma\ (k\ge4),\qquad (\tau_k\sigma)^2=\tau_k\sigma\tau_k\sigma=-\tau_k^2\sigma^2=-1_8, \\
\tau^{O}_h\tau^{O}_k&=\tau_h\tau_k\sigma=-\tau_k\tau_h\sigma=-\tau_k\sigma\tau_h=-\tau^{O}_k\tau^{O}_h,\qquad \tau^{O}_k\tau^{O}_l=\tau_k\sigma\tau_l\sigma=-\tau_k\tau_l=\tau_l\tau_k=-\tau^{O}_l\tau^{O}_k\quad(k\neq l\ge4).
\end{aligned}
```

This is the real counterpart of the complex substitution $`\tau_k\to i\tau_k`$ that turns split octonions into octonions over $`\mathbb C`$.

### 2.2 Octonion vielbein and multiplication constants

Origin: In[294] and In[325] of 4+4.nb with the split spinor metric $`\sigma`$ replaced by the Euclidean $`g^{O}=1_8`$ and the same unit spinor $`u`$:

```math
\begin{aligned}
(E^{O})_{Ba}&=\big(u^{\mathsf T}g^{O}\bar\tau^{O}_B\big)_a,\qquad L^{O}_A=g^{O}_{AA}E^{O}\tau^{O}_A(E^{O})^{-1},\qquad e_Ae_K=\sum_L(L^{O}_A)_{LK}e_L .
\end{aligned}
```

Why the result is the octonion algebra: $`(E^{O})^{-1}e_0=u`$ makes $`e_0`$ a two-sided unit, and $`\tau^{O}(x)\bar\tau^{O}(x)=N(x)1_8`$ together with the orthogonality of $`E^{O}`$ gives $`N(xy)=N(x)N(y)`$. A unital composition algebra of dimension 8 with a positive definite norm is the octonions (Hurwitz). The notebook checks all the laws directly.

### 2.3 Cayley–Dickson form

Origin: Baez 2002, section 2.2. With $`\ell=e_4`$, $`x=a+\ell b`$, $`a=(x_0,\dots,x_3)`$, $`b=(x_4,\dots,x_7)`$:

```math
(a,b)(c,d)=\big(ac+\gamma\,d\bar b,\ \bar a d+cb\big),\qquad \gamma=-1\ (\text{7+1.nb}),\qquad \gamma=+1\ (\text{4+4.nb}).
```

### 2.4 Euclidean mirror $`T^{O}`$

Origin: In[98] of 4+4.nb with $`\tau\to\tau^{O}`$: $`T^{O}_A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\bar\tau^{O}_A\\\tau^{O}_A&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$, so that $`\lbrace T^{O}_A,T^{O}_B\rbrace=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\bar\tau^{O}_A\tau^{O}_B+\bar\tau^{O}_B\tau^{O}_A&0\\0&\tau^{O}_A\bar\tau^{O}_B+\tau^{O}_B\bar\tau^{O}_A\end{smallmatrix}\right)=2\delta_{AB}1_{16}`$. In terms of 4+4.nb: $`T^{O}_A=T_A`$ ($`A\le3`$), $`T^{O}_k=T_kS_{44}`$ ($`k\ge4`$), $`S_{44}=T_4T_5T_6T_7=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma,\sigma)`$.

### 2.5 Real 7+1 generators $`\Gamma^{(1,7)}`$

Origin: the standard construction of a Lorentzian Clifford algebra from a Euclidean one, applied to $`T^{O}`$:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma^{(1,7)}_0&=T^{O}_0,\qquad \Gamma^{(1,7)}_i=T^{O}_0T^{O}_i,\qquad (T^{O}_0T^{O}_i)^2=-(T^{O}_0)^2(T^{O}_i)^2=-1_{16}, \\
\lbrace T^{O}_0,T^{O}_0T^{O}_i\rbrace&=T^{O}_i+T^{O}_0T^{O}_iT^{O}_0=T^{O}_i-T^{O}_i=0,\qquad \lbrace T^{O}_0T^{O}_i,T^{O}_0T^{O}_j\rbrace=-\lbrace T^{O}_i,T^{O}_j\rbrace=-2\delta_{ij}1_{16}.
\end{aligned}
```

Hence $`\lbrace\Gamma^{(1,7)}_A,\Gamma^{(1,7)}_B\rbrace=2\eta^{(1,7)}_{AB}1_{16}`$, and $`\Gamma^{(1,7)}(x)^2=\mathrm{Re}(xx)1_{16}`$.

### 2.6 Complexified generators $`\Gamma^{(7,1)}`$

Origin: the task's instruction to use the complexification of the 4+4 matrices if real numbers fail. Only the time block is multiplied by $`i`$: $`\tau^{C}_0=\bar\tau^{C}_0=i1_8`$, $`\tau^{C}_k=\tau^{O}_k`$; then $`\Gamma^{(7,1)}_A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\bar\tau^{C}_A\\\tau^{C}_A&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$, $`(\Gamma^{(7,1)}_0)^2=i^2\,1_{16}=-1_{16}`$, and $`\Gamma^{(7,1)}_A=S(i\Gamma^{(1,7)}_A)S^{-1}`$ with $`S=(1_{16}-i\Gamma^{(1,7)}_0)/\sqrt2`$.

### 2.7 The impossibility theorem

Origin: Schur's lemma and the uniqueness of the irreducible module of $`Cl_8(\mathbb C)\cong\mathbb C(16)`$. The intertwiners $`M`$ with $`M\overline{\Gamma^{(7,1)}_A}=\Gamma^{(7,1)}_AM`$ are computed in 7+1.nb with `NullSpace`; they form the line through $`M_0=\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1_8\\1_8&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$, and $`M_0\overline{M_0}=-1_{16}`$. A real realization $`R_A=T\Gamma^{(7,1)}_AT^{-1}`$ would give an intertwiner $`M=T^{-1}\overline T`$ with $`M\overline M=1_{16}`$, impossible because $`M=cM_0`$ gives $`M\overline M=-|c|^21_{16}`$.

### 2.8 Real 32×32 generators

Origin: realification. $`\widehat\Gamma^{(7,1)}_A=\varepsilon\otimes\Gamma^{(1,7)}_A`$ with $`\varepsilon^2=-1_2`$ gives $`\lbrace\widehat\Gamma^{(7,1)}_A,\widehat\Gamma^{(7,1)}_B\rbrace=\varepsilon^2\otimes2\eta^{(1,7)}_{AB}1_{16}=2\eta^{(7,1)}_{AB}1_{32}`$. Commutant: $`Q_i=\varepsilon\otimes1_{16}`$, $`Q_j=\sigma_1\otimes\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8`$, $`Q_k=\sigma_3\otimes\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8`$, found from $`\varepsilon\sigma_1=-\sigma_1\varepsilon`$, $`\varepsilon\sigma_3=-\sigma_3\varepsilon`$ and $`\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8\Gamma^{(1,7)}_A=-\Gamma^{(1,7)}_A\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8`$.

### 2.9 Examples

so(1,7): $`\Sigma_{AB}=\frac14[\Gamma^{(1,7)}_A,\Gamma^{(1,7)}_B]`$; the commutation relations follow from the Clifford relation. Boost and rotation: $`(\Gamma^{(1,7)}_0\Gamma^{(1,7)}_1)^2=1_{16}`$, $`(\Gamma^{(1,7)}_4\Gamma^{(1,7)}_5)^2=-1_{16}`$, so $`e^{\beta\Sigma_{01}}=\cosh\frac\beta2+\sinh\frac\beta2\Gamma^{(1,7)}_0\Gamma^{(1,7)}_1`$ and $`e^{\theta\Sigma_{45}}=\cos\frac\theta2+\sin\frac\theta2\Gamma^{(1,7)}_4\Gamma^{(1,7)}_5`$. Dirac operator: $`(\Gamma^A\partial_A)^2=\frac12\lbrace\Gamma^A,\Gamma^B\rbrace\partial_A\partial_B=\eta_{(1,7)}^{AB}\partial_A\partial_B`$. Real amplitudes: $`p=(3,2,2,0,0,0,0,0)`$, $`p^{\mathsf T}\eta^{(1,7)}p=1`$, $`\not p^{\,2}=1_{16}`$; the real matrix $`\not p`$ has a real 8-dimensional eigenspace for the eigenvalue $`+1`$.

Real spinor fields. If $`J`$ anticommutes with every $`\Gamma^{(1,7)}_A`$, then $`J\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8`$ commutes with every $`\Gamma^{(1,7)}_A`$ (because $`\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8`$ anticommutes with each of them), so by irreducibility $`J\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8=c\,1_{16}`$ and $`J=-c\,\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8`$ (as $`(\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8)^{-1}=-\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8`$), whose square is $`-c^2\,1_{16}`$. Hence the real operator $`\Gamma^A\partial_A-mJ`$ has the square $`\partial_0^2-\dots-\partial_7^2-c^2m^2`$ (a tachyon), and a real equation $`(G^A\partial_A-m)\Psi=0`$ implies the Klein–Gordon equation only if the real $`G_A`$ obey the $`\eta^{(7,1)}`$ relation, impossible for 16×16 matrices (2.7). Massless: for $`k=(1,1,0,\dots,0)`$, $`\not k^{\,2}=k^{\mathsf T}\eta^{(1,7)}k\,1_{16}=0`$ and $`\Gamma^A\partial_A[w\cos(k\cdot x)]=-\sin(k\cdot x)\,\not k\,w`$, which vanishes for the 8 real solutions of $`\not k\,w=0`$. Massive, with the real 32×32 matrices: $`P=\sum_Ap^A\widehat\Gamma^{(7,1)}_A`$, $`P^2=-m^2\,1_{32}`$, and with $`\phi=p\cdot x`$, $`\widehat\Gamma^A=\eta_{(7,1)}^{AA}\widehat\Gamma^{(7,1)}_A`$:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\Gamma^{A}\partial_A\Big(w\cos\phi+\tfrac1mPw\sin\phi\Big)&=-P\Big(-w\sin\phi+\tfrac1mPw\cos\phi\Big) \\
&=Pw\sin\phi+m\,w\cos\phi=m\Big(w\cos\phi+\tfrac1mPw\sin\phi\Big),
\end{aligned}
```

using $`\partial_A\phi=(\eta^{(1,7)}p)_A`$ and $`\eta_{(7,1)}^{AA}\eta^{(1,7)}_{AA}=-1`$.

### 2.10 Classification of the nine signatures

For each $`p`$, complex generators $`G_A=T^{O}_A`$ ($`A<p`$), $`G_A=iT^{O}_A`$ ($`A\ge p`$) satisfy $`\lbrace G_A,G_B\rbrace=\pm2\delta_{AB}1_{16}`$ with $`p`$ plus signs. The indicator sign of $`M\overline M`$ ($`M\overline{G_A}=G_AM`$) is $`+1`$ for $`p\in\lbrace8,5,4,1,0\rbrace`$ ($`\mathbb R(16)`$) and $`-1`$ for $`p\in\lbrace7,6,3,2\rbrace`$ ($`\mathbb H(8)`$). ### 6.3 Derivations and decisions

**Dirac matrices.** The real generators $`\Gamma^{(1,7)}_A`$ of section 2.5 are used unchanged: $`\Gamma_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1_8\\1_8&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$, $`\Gamma_i=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\tau^{O}_i&0\\0&-\tau^{O}_i\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$, $`\Gamma_A\Gamma_B+\Gamma_B\Gamma_A=2\eta^{(1,7)}_{AB}1_{16}`$. The opposite sign convention $`\eta^{(7,1)}`$ has no real 16×16 realization (section 2.7), so this is the only real choice. **The factor $`i`$.** With real $`\Gamma_A`$, $`(\Gamma^A\partial_A)^2=\frac12\lbrace\Gamma^A,\Gamma^B\rbrace\partial_A\partial_B=\partial_0^2-\partial_1^2-\dots-\partial_7^2`$, hence

```math
\begin{aligned}
\big(i\Gamma^{A}\partial_A+m\big)\big(i\Gamma^{A}\partial_A-m\big)&=-\big(\partial_0^2-\partial_1^2-\dots-\partial_7^2+m^2\big)1_{16}, \\
\Gamma^0\big(i\Gamma^{A}\partial_A-m\big)&=i\partial_0-H_{\rm D},\qquad H_{\rm D}=-i\Gamma^0\Gamma^i\partial_i+m\Gamma^0 .
\end{aligned}
```

**Real amplitudes.** For $`e^{-i(Ex_0-\vec p\cdot\vec x)}`$, with $`P_\tau=p^i\tau^{O}_i`$ ($`P_\tau^{\mathsf T}=-P_\tau`$, $`P_\tau^2=-\vec p^{\,2}1_8`$):

```math
\begin{aligned}
H_{\rm D}(\vec p)&=\begin{pmatrix}0&m1_8+P_\tau\\m1_8-P_\tau&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad (m1_8+P_\tau)(m1_8-P_\tau)=(m^2+\vec p^{\,2})1_8=E^21_8, \\
H_{\rm D}(\vec p)\begin{pmatrix}E\,e_s\\(m1_8-P_\tau)e_s\end{pmatrix}&=\begin{pmatrix}E^2e_s\\E(m1_8-P_\tau)e_s\end{pmatrix}=E\begin{pmatrix}E\,e_s\\(m1_8-P_\tau)e_s\end{pmatrix}, \\
w_s&=\frac1{\sqrt E}\begin{pmatrix}E\,e_s\\(m1_8-P_\tau)e_s\end{pmatrix},\qquad w_s^{\mathsf T}w_r=2E\,\delta_{sr}.
\end{aligned}
```

**Chirality.** The real matrices anticommuting with every $`\Gamma_A`$ are $`c\,\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8`$ (section 2.9), with square $`-c^21_{16}`$; the chirality is therefore the complex $`\chi=i\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-i1_8\\i1_8&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$. With $`E_\pm`$ the 16×8 matrices $`\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1_8\\-i1_8\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$ and $`\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-i1_8\\1_8\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$ ($`\chi E_-=-E_-`$, $`\chi E_+=E_+`$, $`E_-E_-^\dagger=P^{(1,7)}_L`$, $`E_+E_+^\dagger=P^{(1,7)}_R`$), $`\Psi_L=E_-^\dagger\Psi`$, $`\Psi_R=E_+^\dagger\Psi`$ and

```math
\begin{aligned}
E_-^\dagger\Gamma^0E_+&=E_+^\dagger\Gamma^0E_-=1_8,\qquad E_\mp^\dagger\Gamma^0E_\mp=0,\qquad E_\mp^\dagger\Gamma^0\Gamma^iE_\mp=\mp i\tau^{O}_i,\qquad E_-^\dagger\Gamma^0\Gamma^iE_+=0, \\
E_-^\dagger\Gamma^0\big(i\Gamma^{A}\partial_A-m\big)\Psi&=\big(i\partial_0+\tau^{O}_i\partial_i\big)\Psi_L-m\Psi_R,\qquad E_+^\dagger\Gamma^0\big(i\Gamma^{A}\partial_A-m\big)\Psi=\big(i\partial_0-\tau^{O}_i\partial_i\big)\Psi_R-m\Psi_L .
\end{aligned}
```

**Self-coupling.** The four Lorentz-invariant contact interactions without derivatives used in relativistic point-coupling models (scalar, pseudoscalar, vector, axial vector) with real couplings, and real sources that define the densities:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L&=\bar\Psi\big(i\Gamma^{A}\partial_A-m\big)\Psi-\bar\Psi\big(\mathsf s+i\chi\mathsf p+\Gamma^{A}(\mathsf v_A+\chi\mathsf a_A)\big)\Psi \\
&\quad-\frac{g_S}2(\bar\Psi\Psi)^2-\frac{g_P}2(\bar\Psi i\chi\Psi)^2-\frac{g_V}2(\bar\Psi\Gamma^{A}\Psi)(\bar\Psi\Gamma_A\Psi)-\frac{g_A}2(\bar\Psi\Gamma^{A}\chi\Psi)(\bar\Psi\Gamma_A\chi\Psi).
\end{aligned}
```

The tensor and higher-rank channels are not included. With $`\hbar=c=1`$ every $`g`$ has mass dimension $`-6`$ (an effective theory: no-sea approximation, or a momentum cutoff for the sea). **Hohenberg–Kohn.** For $`H[w]=H_0+H_{\rm int}+\int w\cdot n`$ with $`w\cdot n=\mathsf s\rho_S+\mathsf p\rho_P+\mathsf v_Aj^A+\mathsf a_Aj_5^A`$, two sources $`w\neq w'`$ with different ground states and the same densities would give $`E<E'+\int(w-w')\cdot n`$ and $`E'<E+\int(w'-w)\cdot n`$, so $`E+E'<E+E'`$; hence $`E[w]=\min_n(F[n]+\int w\cdot n)`$. **Kohn–Sham.** Varying $`T_s+\int w\cdot n+E_H+E_{xc}`$ with respect to $`\Phi_k^\dagger`$ under $`\int\Phi_k^\dagger\Phi_l=\delta_{kl}`$ (multipliers $`\lambda_k`$), with $`\delta\rho_S/\delta\Phi^\dagger=\Gamma^0\Phi`$, $`\delta\rho_P/\delta\Phi^\dagger=\Gamma^0i\chi\Phi`$, $`\delta j^A/\delta\Phi^\dagger=\Gamma^0\Gamma^A\Phi`$, $`\delta j_5^A/\delta\Phi^\dagger=\Gamma^0\Gamma^A\chi\Phi`$, gives $`H_{\rm KS}\Phi_k=\lambda_k\Phi_k`$ with

```math
\begin{aligned}

\mathcal V_A&=\mathsf v_A+g_Vj_A+\frac{\delta E_{xc}}{\delta j^A},\qquad \mathcal A_A=\mathsf a_A+g_Aj_{5A}+\frac{\delta E_{xc}}{\delta j_5^A},
\end{aligned}
```

where $`E_H=\int[\frac{g_S}2\rho_S^2+\frac{g_P}2\rho_P^2+\frac{g_V}2j^Aj_A+\frac{g_A}2j_5^Aj_{5A}]`$ and $`T_s=\sum_k\int\Phi_k^\dagger H_{\rm D}\Phi_k`$. **Decision (no sea):** the orbitals are the occupied positive-energy states. **Exchange.** Wick’s theorem for a Slater determinant, $`\langle c_a^\dagger c_c^\dagger c_dc_b\rangle=\varrho_{ba}\varrho_{dc}-\varrho_{da}\varrho_{bc}`$ with $`\varrho_{xy}=\sum_k\Phi_{k,x}\Phi_{k,y}^*`$ and $`\mathcal R=\varrho\,\Gamma^0`$, gives $`\langle:(\bar\Psi O_1\Psi)(\bar\Psi O_2\Psi):\rangle=\mathrm{tr}(O_1\mathcal R)\mathrm{tr}(O_2\mathcal R)-\mathrm{tr}(O_1\mathcal RO_2\mathcal R)`$, hence the exact exchange energy

```math
E_x=-\frac{g_S}2\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal R\mathcal R)-\frac{g_P}2\mathrm{tr}(i\chi\mathcal R\,i\chi\mathcal R)-\frac{g_V}2\mathrm{tr}(\Gamma^{A}\mathcal R\Gamma_A\mathcal R)-\frac{g_A}2\mathrm{tr}(\Gamma^{A}\chi\mathcal R\Gamma_A\chi\mathcal R).
```

**Chiral Kohn–Sham.** With $`\Phi_{kL}=E_-^\dagger\Phi_k`$, $`\Phi_{kR}=E_+^\dagger\Phi_k`$ and the blocks above, $`\kappa=\sum_k\Phi_{kL}^\dagger\Phi_{kR}`$, $`\rho_S=\kappa+\kappa^*`$, $`\rho_P=i(\kappa-\kappa^*)`$, $`\mathcal M=m+\mathcal S+i\mathcal P`$, $`\mathcal V^{L,R}_A=\mathcal V_A\mp\mathcal A_A`$:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\big[\mathcal V^L_0-\tau^{O}_i\partial_i-i\tau^{O}_i\mathcal V^L_i\big]\Phi_L+\mathcal M\Phi_R&=\lambda\Phi_L, \\
\mathcal M^*\Phi_L+\big[\mathcal V^R_0+\tau^{O}_i\partial_i+i\tau^{O}_i\mathcal V^R_i\big]\Phi_R&=\lambda\Phi_R, \\
e^{i\vartheta\chi}&=\cos\vartheta\,1_{16}-\sin\vartheta\,\Gamma^{(1,7)}_8:\qquad \kappa\to e^{2i\vartheta}\kappa, \\
\Delta E_H&=\frac{g_S-g_P}2\big[(e^{4i\vartheta}-1)\kappa^2+(e^{-4i\vartheta}-1)\kappa^{*2}\big].
\end{aligned}
```

**Uniform matter.** Units $`m=1`$; all 8 spin states filled to $`k_F`$; $`|S^6|=16\pi^3/15`$ is the area of the unit sphere in $`\mathbb R^7`$ and $`c_7=8|S^6|/(2\pi)^7=1/(15\pi^4)`$. With the energy density $`\mathcal E`$, its kinetic part $`\mathcal E_{\rm kin}`$, $`\rho_S`$ and the density $`\rho`$:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=\frac{k_F^7}{105\pi^4},\qquad \rho_S=c_7\int_0^{k_F}\frac{k^6m^*\,dk}{\sqrt{k^2+m^{*2}}},\qquad \mathcal E_{\rm kin}=c_7\int_0^{k_F}k^6\sqrt{k^2+m^{*2}}\,dk, \\
m^*&=m+g_S\rho_S(m^*),\qquad \mathcal E=\mathcal E_{\rm kin}-\frac{(m^*-m)^2}{2g_S}+\frac{g_V}2\rho^2, \\
E/A&=\frac{\mathcal E}{\rho},\qquad E_F=g_V\rho+\sqrt{k_F^2+m^{*2}} .
\end{aligned}
```

Because $`\partial\mathcal E/\partial m^*=0`$ is the gap equation, $`d\mathcal E/d\rho=E_F`$ at every density, and $`\frac{d}{dk_F}\frac{\mathcal E}\rho=\frac{c_7k_F^6}{\rho}(E_F-\frac{\mathcal E}{\rho})`$ vanishes at saturation: $`E/A=E_F`$ (Hugenholtz–Van Hove). **Decisions:** $`g_S=-10^5`$, $`g_V=10^4`$, $`g_P=g_A=0`$ (binding and saturation); root finding by Brent’s method with 50-digit (gap equation) and 40-digit (saturation) working precision. **Dirac sea.** With a cutoff $`\Lambda_{\rm c}`$, $`g_S=g_P=g`$ and a uniform $`\mathcal M`$:

```math
\begin{aligned}
J(M)&=\frac1{120\pi^4}\int_0^{\Lambda_{\rm c}}\frac{q^6dq}{\sqrt{q^2+M^2}},\qquad K(M)=\frac1{120\pi^4}\int_0^{\Lambda_{\rm c}}q^6\sqrt{q^2+M^2}\,dq, \\
\kappa&=-4\mathcal M^*J(|\mathcal M|),\qquad \mathcal M=m-8gJ(|\mathcal M|)\mathcal M,\qquad g_c=-\frac1{8J(0)}=-\frac{90\pi^4}{\Lambda_{\rm c}^6}, \\
\mathcal E_{\rm vac}&=-8K+8J\,\mathrm{Re}[(\mathcal M-m)\mathcal M^*]+32g|\mathcal M|^2J^2 .
\end{aligned}
```

**Decisions:** $`\Lambda_{\rm c}=1`$, $`g=\frac65g_c`$, and $`m=\frac1{100}`$ for the explicit-breaking example.
